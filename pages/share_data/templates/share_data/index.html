Share Data

It is recommended that data is shared in a data-type specific, well-recognised repository wherever possible. This will both maximise findability, and should increase the chances that the metadata shared with the data will enable reuse. This page details recommendations on where to share particular types of data. For tailored advice and support, please contact us. Your data can be deposited to the SciLifeLab Data Repository which is run by the SciLifeLab Data Centre (submissions accepted from all life science researchers in Sweden).For more information on storing data, check SciLifeLab FAIR Storage that provides Swedish researchers with secure, high-performance storage designed for large-scale life science datasets, including outputs like sequencing, imaging, and proteomics.

Read the national guidelines for more background information about Open Science in Sweden.

For more general RDM advice relevant to Swedish research, we recommend visiting SciLifeLab Research Data Management Guidelines, and emailing data-management@scilifelab.se for direct support. Check SciLifeLab Data Stewardship Wizard (DSW) an interactive online tool that researchers through the creation of Data Management Plans (DMPs).

Infectious Biology Resources

Infection biology research often involves sensitive, complex, and large-scale data—from genomic sequences of pathogens to clinical metadata and immunological readouts. To support good practices in research data management (RDM) and sharing, several resources have been developed that specifically address the needs of infectious disease researchers. Effective research data management in infection biology relies not only on good planning but also on access to the right infrastructure and expert support.

Most life science data types can be published as raw or processed data in repositories at the EMBL-EBI. When no archive is suitable, use a general purpose repository such as the SciLifeLab Data Repository (submissions accepted from all life science researchers in Sweden), Figshare or Zenodo. Besides scientific data, here you can publish documents, presentations, figures, protocols, or other information that you want to make public at any stage in the research process. A publication here is permanent, and provides a Digital Object Identifier, DOI.

Check our repositories page and data sources page to browse avenues to share and access data. For available datasets refer to get data. For useful resources and tools refer to our Tools page. If you need anaylses or specific instruments then see the SciLifeLab Service Catalog or visit our Services page to view a catalog of available services.

Infectious Disease Toolkit (IDTk)

The Infectious Disease Toolkit (IDTk) is a resource developed to provide practical guidance and community-driven best practices for managing data in infectious disease research. It covers topics such as biosafety, ethical considerations, and data flows for handling sensitive pathogen and patient-derived data. IDTk also links to tools, repositories, and policies relevant to Sweden, making it directly applicable for researchers working with clinical cohorts, sequencing of pathogens, or surveillance data.

Metadata Standards

The following table provides an overview of key metadata standards separated by methodological areas.

Metadata
Standards Description
Genomics MIxS (Minimum Information about any (x) Sequence) Developed by Genomic Standards Consortium ; used for describing sequences from different environments (e.g., host-associated, environmental).
MINSEQE (Minimum Information about a High-Throughput Nucleotide Sequencing Experiment) Recommended by FGED for RNA-seq and other sequencing metadata.
ENA Checklists Specific checklists for submission to European Nucleotide Archive (e.g., pathogen, human, metagenome).
ISA-Tab / ISA-JSON Framework for describing experimental metadata, often used with bioinformatics tools and databases.
Proteomics MIAPE (Minimum Information About a Proteomics Experiment) Developed by HUPO-PSI; covers mass spectrometry, sample processing, informatics.
PSI-MI XML / MITAB For molecular interaction data formats (used in interaction databases).
mzML / mzIdentML / mzTab Standard formats for raw data, identifications, vocabulary and quantification results in the field of mass spectrometry-based proteomics.
Imaging OME-TIFF / OME-XML Developed by Open Microscopy Environment; widely used for storing microscopy images and associated metadata.
REMBI (Recommended Metadata for Biological Images) Designed to enable reproducibility and data reuse for imaging datasets.
DICOM (Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine) Standard for handling, storing, and transmitting medical imaging information (e.g., CT, MRI).
Bioassays / Experimental Data MIACA (Minimum Information About a Cellular Assay) For reporting cellular assays, including experimental context and protocols.
MIABE (Minimum Information About a Bioactive Entity) For small molecule screening and bioactivity reporting.
BAO (BioAssay Ontology) Ontology that enables uniform annotation of bioassays and protocols.
Clinical & Health Data CDISC (e.g., SDTM, ADaM, SEND) Industry standard for clinical trial data exchange and analysis. (not so open)
HL7 / FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) Widely adopted in EHR systems for structured health data.
LOINC / SNOMED CT / ICD-10 Controlled vocabularies for lab tests, symptoms, diagnoses.
MIMIC-IV Metadata Guidelines For structured ICU/clinical datasets in open research.
Dublin Core + DCAT-AP-SE Metadata cataloging for health data in national repositories.
Omics Imaging (e.g., Spatial Transcriptomics, Multi-modal) STOMIC (Spatial Transcriptomics Open Metadata and Image Convention) A proposed standard for organizing spatial omics data.
ISA-Tab + OME-XML For integrating omics and imaging data.
HUPO-B/D Standards For multimodal single-cell data and proteogenomics metadata.
Metabolomics Data ISA-Tab / ISA-JSON Describes experimental design, sample preparation, and data files.
Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) Offers domain-specific guidelines for metadata and reporting.

Licensing

When depositing data, selecting the right license ensures that your work can be reused appropriately while protecting your rights as a data creator. Clear licensing helps others understand what they can do with your data. A data license:

  • Defines how others may access, use, and distribute your data.
  • Ensures you receive proper credit and attribution.
  • SupportsFAIR principles, making data Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable.
  • Reduces legal uncertainty for both creators and users.

Before selecting a license, check:

  • Institutional or funder requirements (e.g., EU Horizon, SciLifeLab, or Swedish Research Council mandates).
  • Whether your data includes sensitive, personal, or third-party components that cannot be fully open.
  • Any journal or repository-specific policies.

Good practices while depositing data:

  • Include a clear license statement in your metadata and documentation.
  • Cite your data DOI in related publications.
  • Acknowledge collaborators and funding bodies appropriately.
  • When in doubt, consult your institutional data steward or legal team before assigning a license.

Where to Find Repository Licensing Information

Licensing options vary between repositories. To identify what applies:

  1. Check the repository’s policy pages, look under About, Terms of Use, or Data Policy.
  2. Review the upload form, many repositories let you select a license during submission.
  3. Look at a published dataset, most repositories display the license on the dataset landing page.
  4. Consult help or support, contact repository support or your data steward if unsure.
  5. Use re3data.org, Re3data.org lists repositories and their licensing policies.

Examples:

  • Zenodo – supports CC0 and CC BY.
  • Figshare – offers Creative Commons options.
  • ENA/EBI – uses standard open data terms.
